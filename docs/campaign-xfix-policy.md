# Campaign — Keep-or-Reject the Completion-Checking Policy

**Status:** contract draft. To be FROZEN (all numbers below pinned) before any
results are collected for the decision. The compatibility check passed on
2026-09-03: all three xfix tasks ran through Harbor in both configurations and
scored 1.0, so the machinery is sound.

## The decision being made

Keep or reject the one-repair completion-checking policy as a supported
runtime feature of `openrouter-agent-cli`. This is the current milestone in
`docs/roadmap.md`: prove (or fail to prove) that the policy substantially
improves agent output, with a pre-committed bar and a powered, audited
campaign.

## The keep/reject rule (frozen — do not move after results exist)

The policy is kept only if, on the frozen task bank with adequate power, the
policy arm beats the unassisted arm on ALL of:

1. **Effect:** verified pass rate at least **10 percentage points higher**,
   with the task-grouped confidence interval **excluding zero**.
2. **Regressions:** no more than **5 percentage points** of baseline-passing
   tasks turning into failures.
3. **Cost:** median token increase **no more than 25%** over the unassisted
   arm.
4. **Integrity:** zero material probe-vs-final-verifier disagreements, and
   the campaign audit passes.

If any bar is missed, the policy is REJECTED — not re-tested with more tasks
or moved thresholds. The campaign counts as complete whether it passes or
fails.

## The honesty section — why this cannot be a confirmatory campaign yet

The compatibility task set is **too easy and too small**: all three tasks
scored 1.0 in BOTH arms, so there is no headroom and no discriminator. The
task-quality rules in `docs/policy-space.md` require a baseline failure rate
around 50% — tasks the unassisted agent fails about half the time. Before a
confirmatory run:

1. **The hard bank must be assembled** — enough independent tasks at ~50%
   baseline failure (the existing 30–100% failure core from the recorded
   runs, plus harder designs; see `docs/policy-space.md`).
2. **A power analysis must size the campaign** — task count and repeats such
   that a pre-committed effect (≥10 points) is detectable with the planned
   confidence, using paired task outcomes and independent tasks, not many
   repeats of a few tasks.
3. **Tokens must be captured** — Harbor's trials do not record provider
   tokens today. The cost bar (≤25% token increase) needs token accounting:
   Harbor's usage tracking or inserting our capture proxy into the loop.

If the hard bank or power analysis shows the campaign is infeasible within
budget, **do not run a confirmatory campaign and do not claim the policy
works** — the honest outcome is "insufficient evidence," recorded as such.

## Treatment separation and audit

- Each arm runs as its own Harbor job; the job config records the treatment
  (`agents[0]/kwargs/mode`), verified in the compatibility run.
- Harbor trial artifacts are converted into our treatment-separated record
  format, then run through the campaign audit (schema, pairing, treatment
  separation, containment) before any decision is written.
- The policy's verifier evidence never enters a normal leaderboard; the
  assisted arm is labeled and reported separately.

## Known limitations (recorded, not blockers for the decision)

- Harbor's `step_results` is empty for our agent (no trajectory protocol yet);
  the agent's internal steps live in the trial's `ora.txt` log.
- The free Nemotron route is weak confirmatory evidence if OpenRouter changes
  the underlying provider or model; the confirmatory run should pin the route
  and, if possible, use a paid stable model.
- The acceptance command for the policy arm is a user-owned check in the
  workspace (visible to the agent by design); it is not the hidden verifier.
